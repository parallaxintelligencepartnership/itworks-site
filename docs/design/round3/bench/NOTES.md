THE TEST BENCH: the site is a piece of measurement equipment, the wall is its chart recorder, and the badge is the readout on its front panel. Nothing here is a dashboard.
The face is an engraved anodized panel: the wordmark cut into the metal, a recessed badge window behind a bezel, and a bank of mechanical drum counters for found, fixed, accepted and criticals open, with one drum caught mid click.
The day ruler is a real slide rule: a scale divided one tick per day, a red index at day 30, and a glass cursor with a vernier lower rail that travels the beam on scroll progress and hard cuts the lamp from green to amber at the mark. With no scroll timeline support, or reduced motion, the cursor rests on day 30 with the amber lamp lit, which makes the same argument standing still.
State is carried four ways at once: a lamp with glass, a bezel and a visible off state, an engraved word, the badge plate itself, and a shape inside the plate (dot, triangle, square), so the page still reads correctly with all color stripped out.
The wall is a strip chart: fanfold paper with sprocket holes and a red grid, a pen gantry riding above it, and a trace down the left margin whose height is the state, high for current, middle for stale, low for a critical still open.
The empty wall is the machine at rest, pen parked and paper loaded, with the mark where line one will print, instead of a sad empty state sentence.
What gets published is a nine post terminal strip, and the two promises are bat handle toggles locked off under a red guard bar, because a switch that is physically guarded off is harder to forget than a paragraph.
Un-SaaS, part one: no cards, no hover lift, no glow, no bento, no feature grid, no hero plus tagline plus CTA, no gradient used as decoration. Every gradient in here renders a material: brushed panel, the cylinder of a drum, the glass of a lens, the chrome of a cursor.
Un-SaaS, part two: the palette stays the brand teal and acid, but it is lit like a panel under a bench lamp rather than a dark product page, and the only light surface on the whole site is paper that the machine prints on.
If it had to be simpler, cut the vernier rail and the mid click drum offset first, then the sprocket holes and the pen arm (keep the trace), then let the guarded toggles become two engraved lines. Keep the drums, the lamps and the chart paper: those three carry the whole idea.
